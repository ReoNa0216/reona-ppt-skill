# Literature And Narrative For Idea Discussion

Read for literature-driven justification or substantial argument revision. Use the existing claim map and production source; this reference requires no additional worksheet.

## Read before selecting

Use titles and abstracts to screen. Before adopting a paper for a central claim, read its full main text and figures, Methods, legends, and relevant supplements. Reconstruct the author's question, experimental chain, and conditions that change the interpretation. Record the material actually obtained; resolve consequential source gaps before relying on the claim.

For selected evidence, retain the system and conditions, comparison or intervention, operational readout, result, exact panel or passage, link, and role in the argument. Whole-paper relevance does not make every panel suitable. Distinguish direct measurement, intervention, prospective association, and model validation; preserve what the readout actually counts or estimates.

Use independent, complementary studies for broad claims, checking counterexamples and applicability. Coverage follows the claim, not a quota of papers or cell types. Prefer recent strong primary evidence when it fits; honor requested venue or date preferences without replacing a better experiment solely for recency. Transfer of a question or analysis strategy does not establish identical mechanisms or method performance across systems.

## Learn the reference argument

For requested reference pages, identify the question each answers, how their examples relate, and which premise makes the next page follow. Transfer that reasoning pattern where it fits the new evidence. Verify scientific claims independently; keep visual imitation from determining the scientific story.

## Assign evidence to the argument

State each page's role before choosing its papers. Examples may establish functional value, support an intervention, reveal a temporal relationship, compare explanations, or motivate a test. Select roles needed by the actual question.

Make page relationships explicit: progression, overview and subtopics, corroboration, or comparison. An overview must cover its later subtopics. Titles should communicate the scientific relationship being established; use an affirmative judgment when supported and a question when unresolved. Name the relevant object or stage rather than relying on vague references to “these changes.”

For methods literature, explain `input → modeling operation → output → validation → demonstrated answer`, then identify the additional test. Explain unfamiliar statistics through their objects and comparison. Describe existing studies' actual scope; do not dismiss a modality wholesale or append a generic limitation to every paper.

## Select what the audience sees

Use the shared [literature screenshot rules](../SKILL.md#literature-screenshots). Combine studies when they jointly support one judgment; give an experiment more space when the audience must inspect its groups, time order, rescue, or validation to follow the inference. A paper may serve different roles on different pages without repeating its introduction.

Visible text should connect the model, consequential condition or operation, and result to the page's judgment. Keep labels and qualifications necessary to read the evidence. Put supporting background in notes and procedural detail in source records unless it changes interpretation.

Read the titles consecutively, then inspect each title with its evidence and text. Can the listener explain what each example contributes and why the next page follows? Remove sentences whose deletion loses no evidence, reasoning, or necessary context. Route notes through the shared protocol; avoid mechanical “relevance to our project” paragraphs.
