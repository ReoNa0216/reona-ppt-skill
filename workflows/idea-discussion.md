# Workflow: Idea Discussion

Use for scientific directions, proposed models or experiments, and discussions of what is worth testing and how to test it.

## Task and review agreement

- **Discussion only:** investigate, compare feasible options, and recommend a direction. Stay in discussion until production is authorized.
- **New or substantially restructured argument:** prepare the page-and-notes draft described in the [content contract](../references/scientific-content-contract.md#idea-review-draft). Honor an established preference to review this draft before production; ordinary acceptance is sufficient. An explicit request to skip review overrides that preference.
- **Authorized production:** reuse an accepted plan. Without a prior-review agreement, a request to create or revise authorizes production after the necessary planning; no extra approval phrase is required.
- **Focused revision:** update affected content and dependencies, preserving other accepted choices. Reopen a decision only when the request or new evidence materially changes it.

Recover visible pages, notes, sources, and decision history before asking questions. Keep the current agreed design in the existing production source. In a mixed deck, an unresolved choice holds only its dependent work.

Follow the [evidence-first interview](../references/evidence-first-interview.md) for consequential missing information. Continue independent work while answers are pending.

## Establish the question and contribution

Use these questions to develop the argument, not as a required slide sequence:

1. What functional, clinical, scientific, or practical outcome makes the problem worth studying?
2. What has evidence established, and what specific relationship or explanation remains to be tested?
3. Why does the chosen system make that test feasible? Separate the broader question from the available model or material.
4. What is the smallest independently useful test: data object, experimental/analysis unit, input, comparison, output, and resource requirement?
5. What information does the proposed measurement or method add over a credible simpler alternative?
6. Which observed result would support, revise, or reject the hypothesis, and change the next decision?

When a method already exists, distinguish its demonstrated capability from the scientific value and untested performance of a new application. A missing data modality alone is not a research question. Reproducing an established result can anchor validity; identify the additional information before claiming novelty.

Preserve accepted sampling, resource, intervention, and evaluation commitments during narrative revision. Keep method-establishment suggestions distinct from the formal design. Raise a material scientific or resource change with its reason and consequences before replacing the agreed design.

## Build the argument

A useful progression is:

`important outcome → established evidence → unresolved question → proposed measurement and analysis → decisive test → decision`

Adapt it to the meeting. Exploratory discussion may teach evidence and compare explanations; an execution deck foregrounds accepted comparisons, operations, readouts, and dependencies.

For literature-driven justification or substantial narrative reconstruction, read [literature and narrative](../references/idea-literature-narrative.md). It covers full-paper selection, evidence roles, page relationships, and concrete method comparisons. Reuse verified sources for focused edits; expand research when the claim changes.

Record consequential claims using the shared [content contract](../references/scientific-content-contract.md). Keep evidence status and audit concerns in the planning source; display only what helps the audience understand the claim.

## Connect methods to a test

Introduce the scientific object before the named method. Explain its input, operation, output, and use in answering the question. Use a small valid example when the operation or statistic is unfamiliar.

Distinguish demonstrated capability from a proposed extension through assumptions, required data, and validation. Compare performance on comparable tasks; when the contribution is new information or a different target, define its value and its own test. Complexity alone supplies no advantage.

Read [validation design](../references/idea-validation-design.md) for pilot-based scope decisions, prediction, transfer, or model-derived intervention candidates. Preserve training access, held-out units, calibration, and metrics at the level of the actual output.

When several components are proposed, identify which are essential and which depend on preliminary evidence. Link informative preliminary outcomes to scope choices. Retain a combined design when the interaction is the question and can be tested with available resources.

## Draft before production

For argument construction, write the [Idea review draft](../references/scientific-content-contract.md#idea-review-draft) in the existing planning source. Apply the task's review agreement above, then carry the accepted content into production.

Before making affected pages, check:

- titles form a coherent progression or an explicit comparison;
- each displayed example supports its page's judgment;
- visible text supplies the necessary reading cues and omits unrelated findings;
- notes provide the background and reasoning a listener needs;
- the proposed test still matches the agreed design.

If essential reasoning cannot fit legibly, split the explanation or adjust scope with the user. Use backup pages for relevant follow-up questions requiring their own visual, table, or derivation; omit redundant material from the deck while preserving its sources.

## Revise and deliver

Treat an ambiguous figure, unsupported transition, or mismatched readout as a reasoning problem. Repair the explanation or design, then update the affected page, notes, assets, and source together. Preserve accepted content and visual conventions outside that dependency.

Use the shared figure, notes, and final-export rules. Inspect the delivered file at presentation scale, including screenshot context and legibility, and verify protected references remain unchanged. Use Microsoft PowerPoint when the user requires its rendering.

Close with the decision this meeting needs: a recommended design, decisive experiment, validation comparison, or conditional next stage. Reuse the working production pipeline and deliver only the requested formats.
